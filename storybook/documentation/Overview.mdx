<H2>The four tiers</H2>

<P>Every visual decision travels through four tiers. Each tier has one job, and values exist exactly once.</P>

<Table head={['Tier', 'Speaks', 'Job']}>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><C>primitive</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>pigment names</td>
    <td style={S.td}>Raw values only — every colour with its two theme faces. Never an alias to another primitive; the layer is the ground truth.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><C>alias</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>the product palette</td>
    <td style={S.td}>Allocates pigments to product meaning — the brand colours, the neutral, the statuses — and exposes each in every behavior. Pure pointers; no values of its own.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><C>semantic</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>usage roles</td>
    <td style={S.td}>The only tier a designer picks from: text roles, foreground glyphs, borders, surfaces. Each role binds one alias route.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>

    <td style={S.td}>per-control contracts</td>
    <td style={S.td}>Control-specific values (a selected tab, a dial arc) that compose semantic roles into named parts.</td>
  </tr>

<H2>The naming grammar</H2>

<P>
  Every semantic colour token is a sentence built from four orthogonal slots —{' '}
  <C>{'<family>[/group] × <register> × <theme> × <constancy>'}</C> — and no slot ever borrows another
  slot's word. A reader who knows the vocabulary can parse any token without a legend, and mint a missing
  one without inventing vocabulary.
</P>

<Table head={['Slot', 'Words', 'Meaning']}>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><b>family</b></td>
    <td style={S.td}><C>max</C> · <C>primary</C> · <C>brand/…</C> · <C>system/…</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>What is speaking: the extremes, the brand voice, a brand colour, or the platform (statuses and field neutrals).</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><b>register</b></td>
    <td style={S.td}>bare · <C>-strong</C> · <C>-subtle</C> · <C>-muted</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>How loudly: defaults carry no word; strong amplifies (and is the on-tint text voice); subtle supports; muted is the quietest readable tier.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><b>theme</b></td>
    <td style={S.td}>bare · <C>-inverse</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>Which way it follows the world — inverse serves content on surfaces that invert against the theme.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td style={S.td}><b>constancy</b></td>
    <td style={S.td}>bare · <C>-perma</C></td>
    <td style={S.td}>Whether it flips at all: perma freezes what you see in the light theme; inverse-perma freezes what you see in the dark theme.</td>
  </tr>
</Table>

<P>
  Families are picker folders (<C>text/primary/…</C>, <C>text/brand/…</C>, <C>text/system/…</C>). Folders
  are free — code flattens every path to dashes — with two corollaries: the folder word never repeats in
  the leaf, and slash and dash spellings of one path must never coexist.
</P>

<H2>The laws behind the words</H2>

<P>
  <b>Perma is anchored identity, not frozen bytes.</b> Mono families pin to literal values — Ink is Ink
  everywhere. Colour families pin to their anchored voice, dark-tuned in the dark theme, because primitive
  constancy is anchor-by-counting by design. Media that never flips uses <C>max</C>, whose extremes are
  byte-stable.
</P>
<P>
  <b>Registers select values; constancy never does.</b> Pinning a deeper voice is a composition —
  amplify, then pin — never a special-cased word.
</P>
<P>
  <b>One word, one meaning.</b> Ordinal words exist only inside <C>brand/</C> as allocation order;
  supporting tiers are registers of <C>primary</C>; state words never appear in colour tokens — interaction
  is expressed as washes.
</P>
<P>
  <b>Lazy mint, loud gaps.</b> Only consumed combinations exist; the grammar guarantees a missing token's
  name before the token does.
</P>
<P>
  <b>Descriptions are law, not data.</b> Token descriptions state method and usage in neutral prose;
  values live in the tokens, and this documentation renders them from the live pull — the pages cannot
  drift from the file.
</P>

<H2>How theming works</H2>

<P>
  Themes flip at the <b>primitive</b> tier and nowhere else. Primitive colour ramps carry a Light and a
  Dark face; aliases and semantics are single-mode and inherit the flip through the chain. Ink and Snow
  flip into <i>each other</i>, not into Aluminium's endpoints.
</P>

<H2>The neutral-first law</H2>

<P>
  ONEMO's primary identity is Ink, Snow and Aluminium. Coloured ramps are occasional accents — an
  indicator, a dial arc, a focus ring — never the default surface of buttons or icons. The palette is
  built so the neutral system carries the product and colour appears deliberately.
</P>
