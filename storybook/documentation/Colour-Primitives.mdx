<H2>The nine mechanism parents</H2>

<P>
  The collection is organised behavior-first: the theme-following default sits bare (the ramp folders and{' '}
  <C>base/</C>), and every deviation has exactly one parent folder — <C>alpha/</C> for transparency,{' '}
  <C>inverse/</C> for swaps, <C>inverse-alpha/</C> for swapped transparency, <C>constant/</C> and{' '}
  <C>constant-alpha/</C> for pinned forms, <C>inverse-constant/</C> and <C>inverse-constant-alpha/</C> for
  pinned swaps. The mirror rule holds inside every parent: ramps appear by name, pairs under <C>base/</C>,
  and the deepest level is always the numeric ladder. One mechanism per folder — a token's address states
  its behavior.
</P>

<H2>Adaptive families</H2>

<P>
  Each family is a twelve-step scale following the Radix composition: the first steps are backgrounds, the
  middle steps are component fills and borders, the deep steps are solids and text. The two strips per row
  are the same family's light and dark faces — the flip the whole system inherits.
</P>

<AdaptiveFamilies />

<H2>Base pairs</H2>

<P>
  Pair tokens are named by both faces, light-theme face first — <C>ink-snow</C> shows ink in light and snow
  in dark; <C>silver-space</C> is the surface metal; <C>white-black</C> the pure extremes. Their swaps live
  under <C>inverse/base/</C> with the same honest naming reversed. Single-colour names exist only in the
  constant folders, because pinned tokens are the only ones that are one colour. Pairs carry two behavior
  sets (adaptive, pinned-own); a pair's pinned swap would duplicate its partner's constant, so those tokens
  deliberately do not exist — ramps, having no partner, carry all four sets.
</P>

<BaseTones />

<H2>Transparency — <C>alpha/</C></H2>

<P>
  Every solid step has a transparent twin: the most transparent colour that still composites to exactly the
  solid over its background. Twins let one token read correctly over photos, glass and gradients while
  matching the palette on flat surfaces. The proof renders live below — each pill is half solid, half twin
  over the strip's background; a visible seam would mean a mismatch.
</P>

<TwinProof family="grey" />

<H2>Pinned — <C>constant/</C> · <C>inverse-constant/</C></H2>

<P>
  Constants stop a token from following the theme. <C>constant/</C> pins each entry's own reading;{' '}
  <C>inverse-constant/</C> pins the swapped reading. For ramps, pinning is anchored identity rather than
  frozen bytes — a step keeps its position and re-tunes per world; the pair constants pin literal values.
</P>

<AnchorDemo family="grey" />

<H2>Pinned transparency — <C>constant-alpha/</C> · <C>inverse-constant-alpha/</C></H2>

<P>
  The transparent counterparts of the constants: ladders that keep the same reading when the theme flips —
  the workhorses for scrims and photo overlays, where the imagery underneath never theme-flips.
</P>

<AlphaLadder path="constant-alpha/base/black" />

<H2>Swapped — <C>inverse/</C> · <C>inverse-alpha/</C></H2>

<P>
  The adaptive world with its faces exchanged: the light face carries the dark value and vice versa. Ramp
  swaps and pair swaps live here, with the swapped pairs' transparency under <C>inverse-alpha/</C>. This is
  mechanical inversion — whether an inversion counts as contrast is a semantic-tier judgment, made one tier
  up, never here.
</P>
