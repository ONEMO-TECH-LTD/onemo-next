<H2>The pair naming law</H2>

<P>
  A base token holds two theme faces, which means it IS a pair — and naming it after one member is how
  confusion breeds. So adaptive pair tokens carry both faces in reading order: <C>ink-snow</C> shows ink
  in the light theme and snow in the dark. Each pair's swap is a separate token in the{' '}
  <C>inverse/</C> section (<C>snow-ink</C>, <C>space-silver</C>, <C>black-white</C>) — same values, faces
  reversed. Single-colour names are reserved for the pinned tokens (<C>constant/base/ink</C>), because
  a pinned token is the only kind that is truly one colour.
</P>

<BaseMaterials />

<H2>What each pair is</H2>

<P>
  <C>ink-snow</C> is the voice pair — the brand black and the paper it prints on; the UI's default
  text world. <C>silver-space</C> is the surface pair — the metal slab the editor stands on, silver in
  the light world, space in the dark. <C>white-black</C> is the pure pair — the absolute extremes,
  reserved for media and the max reserve. Pairs carry two behavior sets (adaptive and pinned-own);
  their swapped-pinned forms are the partner's constants, so those tokens deliberately do not exist.
</P>

<H2>Where they surface</H2>

<P>
  The editor ground binds the surface pair (<C>bg/app/primary</C>); its opposing and pinned readings ride
  the same pair's swap and constant. The voice pair carries text and the flipping brand surfaces; the pure
  pair carries the max reserve. The grain treatment for the anodized look remains a screen-level texture
  over the token — the tier owns the tint, the node owns the noise.
</P>
