<H2>The pairs</H2>

<P>
  A base material is a primitive with two theme faces. Ink and Snow are stored as mirror images of each
  other — each one's dark face is the other's light face — so for that pair, "inverse" needs no token:
  the partner is the inverse. Aluminium is the third material, the anodized slab the editor stands on.
  It is theme-tuned rather than mirrored — its faces do not swap into any partner — so it is the one
  base pair that stores its swap explicitly, in the <C>inverse/</C> section, the same way the ramps do.
</P>

<H2>The aluminium behavior grid</H2>

<P>
  Every base material carries the same grid, and aluminium's is complete: the light- and dark-anchored
  constants (what <C>-perma</C> and <C>-inverse-perma</C> resolve through), the adaptive alpha ladder
  (its own face per theme), the pinned light and dark alpha ladders, and the explicit inverse. Missing
  eventualities — washes on the swapped face — mint lazily at their predictable names.
</P>

<H2>Where it surfaces</H2>

<P>
  The editor ground binds the aluminium route (<C>bg/app/primary</C>); the ordinal canvases beneath it
  step through the grey ramp. Snow returns to what it is in the brand language — the paper material for
  surfaces that want Snow — and Ink keeps the voice. The grain treatment for the anodized look is a
  screen-level texture over the token, not a colour: the tier owns the tint, the node owns the noise.
</P>
