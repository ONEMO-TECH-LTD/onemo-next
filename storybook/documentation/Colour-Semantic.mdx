<H2>The grammar</H2>

<P>
  <b>Family</b> says what is speaking: the brand voice (<C>primary</C> — Ink on Snow), the pure extremes
  (<C>max</C>), the platform (<C>system</C>), or a brand colour (<C>brand/…</C>). <b>Register</b> says how
  loudly — <C>-strong</C> amplifies, <C>-subtle</C> quiets, <C>-muted</C> is the quietest readable tier.{' '}
  <b>Theme</b> says which way it follows the world — a bare name follows the theme, <C>-inverse</C> opposes
  it. <b>Constancy</b> says whether it flips at all: <C>-perma</C> freezes what you see in the light theme,{' '}
  <C>-inverse-perma</C> freezes what you see in the dark theme. Perma composes with any family, register or
  theme word — a missing constant is minted by suffixing, never by inventing vocabulary.
</P>

<P>
  Families are picker folders: variants live inside <C>text/primary/…</C>, <C>text/brand/…</C>,{' '}
  <C>text/max/…</C>, <C>text/system/…</C>, and the glyph tier mirrors the shape. Folders are free — code
  flattens every path to dashes — so the folder word never repeats in the leaf, and slash and dash
  spellings of one path must never coexist.
</P>

<P>
  Every sample below renders on a surface derived from the role's own name — pinned readings on the
  opposing material, inverse on the flipping control, extremes on imagery — left panel light theme, right
  panel dark.
</P>

<H2>fg/ — the glyph tier</H2>

<P>
  Non-text glyphs — icon strokes, indicator dots, tick lines — mirror the text tier combination for
  combination, on the same alias routes. The tier law: <b>glyphs follow text wherever they share a
  surface; they diverge only where optics differ</b> — the non-text contrast floor, the muted tier, and
  the solid graphic register.
</P>
