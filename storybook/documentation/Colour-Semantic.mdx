<H2>The grammar</H2>

<P>
  <b>Family</b> says what is speaking: the brand voice (<C>primary</C> — ink on snow), the semantic
  reserve (<C>max</C> — extremes for cases not yet named; known contexts own their own words), the
  platform (<C>system</C>), or a brand colour (<C>brand/…</C>). <b>Register</b> says how loudly —{' '}
  <C>default</C>, <C>-strong</C>, <C>-subtle</C>, <C>-muted</C> — and the register is always spoken:
  the default's variants carry the <C>default-</C> prefix exactly as <C>subtle-contrast</C> carries
  subtle. Registers select steps — the quiet tiers ride grey steps, never transparency tricks; alphas
  appear only where the role itself is a see-through layer (materials, washes, scrims). <b>Theme</b>{' '}
  says which way it follows the world, with a condition on the word: <C>-contrast</C> is opposition on
  mono routes, where a swap genuinely delivers the contrast function; <C>-inverse</C> is opposition on
  colour routes, where the swap is theme tuning and a contrast claim would lie. <b>Constancy</b>:{' '}
  <C>-perma</C> pins whichever reading it suffixes — <C>default-perma</C> freezes the light-world
  reading, <C>contrast-perma</C> the opposing one. A missing combination is minted by composing these
  words, never by inventing vocabulary.
</P>

<P>
  Families are picker folders: variants live inside <C>text/primary/…</C>, <C>text/brand/…</C>,{' '}
  <C>text/max/…</C>, <C>text/system/…</C>, and the glyph tier mirrors the shape. Folders are free — code
  flattens every path to dashes — so the folder word never repeats in the leaf, and slash and dash
  spellings of one path must never coexist.
</P>

<P>
  Every sample below renders on a surface derived from the role's own name — pinned readings on the
  opposing material, inverse on the flipping control, extremes on imagery — left panel light theme, right
  panel dark.
</P>

<H2>fg/ — the glyph tier</H2>

<P>
  Non-text glyphs — icon strokes, indicator dots, tick lines — mirror the text tier combination for
  combination, on the same alias routes. The tier law: <b>glyphs follow text wherever they share a
  surface; they diverge only where optics differ</b> — the non-text contrast floor, the muted tier, and
  the solid graphic register.
</P>
