<H2>The grammar</H2>

<P>
  One order everywhere: <C>colour/behavior/step</C>. A bare step is the default — the adaptive solid that
  follows the theme. Ramp families nest their behaviors as folders (<C>neutral/constant/…</C>); pair and
  face entries compose with dashes instead (<C>brand/ink-snow-alpha/…</C>, <C>brand/ink-constant</C>) —
  no word-folder ever sits below the family, and the deepest level is always the numeric ladder. Asking
  for any behavior of any palette colour is a predictable path, never a new name.
</P>

<H2>The brand</H2>

<P>
  The brand group holds the voice pair, the surface pair, their pinned faces, and the numbered brand
  colours. Numbering is deliberate: a new brand colour joins as the next number with nothing else moving —
  numbers say palette identity while the semantic tier speaks role language on top.
</P>

<H2>Why this tier is invisible in the picker</H2>

<P>
  Primitives and aliases are plumbing: they carry no scopes, so they never appear where a designer chooses a
  fill. The picker offers only the semantic tier — the roles — keeping every selection a usage decision
  rather than a colour decision. The full cascade still resolves underneath.
</P>

<Note>
  Behavior qualifiers on a colour resolve to the matching primitive group of its pigment — e.g.{' '}
  <C>brand/1/inverse/…</C> points into <C>inverse/…</C> of the pigment family the brand colour is allocated
  to.
</Note>
