<H2>The grammar</H2>

<P>
  Every route reads <C>colour/qualifier/step</C>. A bare step is the default — the adaptive solid that
  follows the theme. Behavior qualifiers nest inside each colour, the same six behaviors the primitives
  define, so asking for a transparent or anchored form of any palette colour is a predictable path, never a
  new name.
</P>

<H2>The brand</H2>

<P>
  The brand group holds Ink and Snow and the numbered brand colours. Numbering is deliberate: a new
  brand colour joins as the next number with nothing else moving, and the numbers say palette identity
  while the semantic tier speaks role language on top.
</P>

<H2>Why this tier is invisible in the picker</H2>

<P>
  Primitives and aliases are plumbing: they carry no scopes, so they never appear where a designer chooses a
  fill. The picker offers only the semantic tier — the roles — keeping every selection a usage decision
  rather than a colour decision. The full cascade still resolves underneath.
</P>

<Note>
  Behavior qualifiers on a colour resolve to the matching primitive group of its pigment — e.g.{' '}
  <C>brand/1/inverse/…</C> points into <C>inverse/…</C> of the pigment family the brand colour is allocated
  to.
</Note>
