How this is built

Structure

Each Effect face is three nested layers: an outer rim (the brushed-metal frame), an inner well (the recessed cavity, clipped with overflow:hidden), and the content (image on the front, solid colour on the back). No 3D engine — the depth is pure CSS.

The metal frame illusion

The rim's roundness + dimensionality is a stack of layered inset box-shadows: bright top/left insets read as light hitting a bevel, dark bottom insets read as the shadowed underside. A diagonal linear-gradient over the base colour gives the anodised sheen. The well repeats the trick inverted (dark top inset, light bottom) so it reads as carved into the surface.

Suede / nap material

A single tiled noise PNG (grain1x.png) is overlaid twice on each face:

  • Nap — the grain at the chosen blend mode (soft-light by default): lightens & darkens per-pixel for the velvety tooth.
  • Depth — the same grain again in multiply, darkening only, so it reads as printed into the texture (front only).

Grain scale changes the tile background-size (fine suede → coarse canvas). Sheen is a soft top-left radial-gradient highlight.

Per-face independence

Front and back hold separate material state ({nap, depth, sheen, grain, blend}). The Editing toggle just points the sliders at one object or the other — so a light front image keeps its own settings and isn't darkened by the back's. The active face shows a blue selection ring.

The drifting highlight

A conic-gradient of three soft arcs sits behind the rim. A registered @property --sweep (an <angle>) is animated 0→360°, and the gradient reads from var(--sweep) — so the light rotates around the frame. Colour, speed, and on/off are state-driven; turning it off sets animation:none.

Performance note

The continuously-animating conic and the stacked mix-blend-mode layers are the heaviest parts (they repaint every frame / force compositing). On mobile, ship with the sweep paused-when-idle and consider baking the grain into a static texture rather than two live blend layers.
